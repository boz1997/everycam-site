QR code wedding photo album: how it works

Updated October 2026 · 6-minute read

Every wedding is photographed a hundred times over, by a hundred phones. The problem was never taking the pictures. It was getting them back. A QR code photo album fixes that with one printed card per table: guests scan it, type their name, and their photos land in a single live album that the couple actually owns.

What a QR code photo album is

It is a shared album with a door. The door is a QR code (and a short text code for anyone whose camera won’t cooperate). Behind it sits one gallery for the whole event. Nobody installs anything, nobody creates an account, nobody has to find the couple on a messaging app three weeks later. The photos go straight from the guest’s phone to the album, with the guest’s name attached.

With ShareCam the album lives at sharecam.app/join. The host creates the event in the iPhone app or on the web dashboard, picks a package, and gets a 6-character code and a QR code that opens the album in any phone browser.

Setting it up: five minutes

  1. Create the event. Name, date, and whether the album is open (everyone sees everything live) or private (guests upload blind, you reveal later).
  2. Choose a package. Every event starts free. Paid packages are a one-time purchase for that event, nothing renews. The package sets how many photos, guests and videos the album holds and how long it is kept.
  3. Print the table cards. ShareCam ships 16 card designs with the QR and the code on them, four per A4 sheet with cut marks. Put one on each table and one near the bar.
  4. Tell guests once. The officiant, the DJ or the menu card: “scan the code on your table, your photos go into our album.” That one sentence does most of the work.
  5. Watch it fill. On Unlimited you can also put the album on the venue’s screen as a live photo wall, so guests see their shots appear.

Open or private?

Open is a party: guests see the album grow during the night, like photos, and find themselves in other people’s pictures. Private mode is the disposable-camera feeling without the chemicals: guests upload without seeing anyone else’s photos, and the couple reveals the whole night at once, by hand or at a time they set. Switching from private to open is a one-way reveal, so the surprise stays intact.

What guests actually experience

They point the camera at the card, a page opens, they type a first name, and they pick photos or videos from their camera roll. Photos are resized on the phone before upload so they go up quickly on venue Wi-Fi; on the Unlimited package the full-resolution original is kept as well. Videos up to two minutes are welcome on Party and above. If someone forgets to upload on the night, the code keeps working for the whole storage window.

Keeping control

Every upload carries a name, so the host sees who added what. Hosts can hide or delete any photo, remove a guest, and pause new joins once the night is over. Guests can report a photo and it goes straight to the host, not to a moderation queue somewhere else.

Getting the photos out

One tap saves the whole album to the host’s phone, or builds a ZIP for the laptop. On Unlimited the host can also download the guests’ original files from a computer, in ZIP parts. Storage runs from 7 days on the free package to a year on Unlimited, counted from the event date, with a reminder before it ends.

What it costs

The first event is free (50 photos, 10 guests, a week of storage) which is enough to try it on a dinner. For a wedding, most couples pick a package sized to the guest count. Prices are one-time, per event, and listed on the pricing page.

Try it on your own event. Create a free event in the ShareCam iPhone app or on the web dashboard, print the QR card, and see the album fill up. Paid packages are one-time, per event.
