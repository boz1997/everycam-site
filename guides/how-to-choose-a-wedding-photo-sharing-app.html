How to choose a wedding photo sharing app: 9 things to check

Also in Deutsch · Español · Français

Updated October 2026 · 6-minute read

There are dozens of apps that promise to collect your guests’ photos. They differ less in features than in friction, and friction is what decides whether you end up with 800 photos or 40. Here is the checklist we would use, in the order it matters.

1. What does a guest have to do?

Scan, type a name, upload: that is the ceiling. If the guest has to install an app, create an account or verify an email, expect most of the table to drop out. Check that it works on Android as well as iPhone, since your guests will have both. ShareCam’s guests use the phone browser at sharecam.app/join; only the host needs the app or the dashboard.

2. Is there a name on every upload?

Anonymous uploads sound friendly and become a problem the first time you want the original of one photo or need to remove a guest. A name per upload costs guests two seconds and saves you hours.

3. Open gallery, private reveal, or both?

Parties want a live shared gallery. Weddings often want the disposable-camera surprise: guests upload blind, the couple reveals later. The useful apps do both and let you schedule the reveal.

4. Who controls the album?

You should be able to hide or delete any photo, remove a guest, and stop new joins after the night. Guests should be able to report a photo to you, not to a help desk.

5. How long is it kept, and how are you told?

Look for a stated window per package, counted from the event date, with a reminder before it ends. Silent deletion is the failure mode to avoid. ShareCam keeps albums from 7 days (free) to a year (Unlimited) and notifies the host before storage ends.

6. How do you get everything out?

One-tap save to the phone and a ZIP for the laptop are the minimum. If originals matter to you, check whether the service keeps them: most resize phone photos for speed, which is right for the live album but not for a print. On ShareCam the Unlimited package keeps the guests’ full-resolution originals as well, and photographer packages always keep originals.

7. Videos?

The first dance and the speeches are video. Check the limit per clip and per album. ShareCam takes videos of up to two minutes on Party and above, with no file size cap, and any length on the top photographer package.

8. Subscription or one-time?

A wedding is one day. A one-time price per event is the honest model: you pay for that album and its storage window, and nothing renews. Be wary of monthly plans you will forget to cancel.

9. Printed cards and a live wall

The technology is useless if nobody scans. Ready-made table cards (so you are not designing QR cards the week of the wedding) and a live wall on the venue screen are the two features that actually move the upload count. ShareCam ships 16 card designs and includes the wall with Unlimited.

A quick way to test any app

Create a free event, print one card, and hand it to the least technical person you know at dinner. If their photo is in the album before dessert, you have found your app.

Try it on your own event. Create a free event in the ShareCam iPhone app or on the web dashboard, print the QR card, and see the album fill up. Paid packages are one-time, per event.
